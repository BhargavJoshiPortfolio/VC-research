# Phase 1 Shortlist (v2): 10 European Pre-Seed Companies

*Compiled 18 September 2026. Every fact comes from press coverage of the funding round or the company's own announcement (linked per company). None of it is verified against primary sources yet; that is Phase 2. Items marked **[Analyst view]** are my preliminary judgement, not reported fact.*

**What changed in v2.** The first list leaned toward AI and software. This version spreads across biotech, robotics, agriculture, foodtech, services-based models, energy, space, semiconductors and fintech. Only two of the ten (Tellia, Backbone) are AI-native products. **v2.1** swapped NextGO Epi and finperks for Klona Biotech (medtech-adjacent) and Mycoverse (foodtech-adjacent). The companies I dropped are kept in the [reserve list](#reserve-list-from-the-first-pass) below.

## Screening criteria

1. Announced a pre-seed round in 2026 (Telura raised in autumn 2025 and announced in March 2026).
2. Europe-headquartered or Europe-founded.
3. Technology or product is the core of the story, not just a market gap.
4. Plausibly relevant to both European and US investors.
5. Sector diversity: no two companies solve the same problem.

"Pre-seed" is a loose label in 2026. Rounds here run from $2M to $7.2M, and several teams already have paying customers or pilots.

## 1. Klona Biotech (London, UK) · Medtech-adjacent: regenerative implants

**What it does.** A bioprinting platform for patient-specific implants for mechanically demanding tissue (ligaments, tendons, cartilage). It prints fibre-reinforced "gel-textile" composites that the company says are up to 20 times stronger than standard bioprints while staying compatible with living cells. The longer-term aim is printers that hospitals use to make implants on demand. First product in development is a ligament implant.

**Problem.** Millions of patients a year need grafts for these tissues, and donor shortages and graft failure limit recovery.

**Team.** Dylan Yeo (Oxford) and Thomas Groom (Imperial College London), founded 2025. Part of SOSV's IndieBio programme.

**Round.** $1.4M pre-seed led by Draper Associates, with Arben Ventures, IndieBio, Outset Ventures and SOSV. Announcement date not confirmed; a funding tracker lists it in Q1 2026. To verify.

**Why it made the list.** A genuine medtech and regenerative-medicine play with a US lead investor already on board, which speaks to the US-fit criterion. Hospital point-of-care manufacturing is a distinctive angle.

**Open questions.** No preclinical or clinical data in the coverage I found. Regulatory route (an implant combined with cell-containing material is a hard class). Mechanical claims are the company's own. The "decentralised in hospitals" model adds regulatory and quality-control burden. Smallest round on the list, so a seed raise is likely soon.

**Sources.** [SOSV profile](https://sosv.com/company/klona-biotech/) · [Draper portfolio](https://www.draper.vc/portfolio/klona-biotech) · [Company site](https://klonabiotech.com/)

## 2. Immitra Bio (Switzerland) · Biotech

**What it does.** A platform for one-time, curative in-vivo gene editing. It combines a mutation-agnostic editing technology, a digital target-identification system and non-viral delivery, aiming to avoid ex-vivo cell manipulation, patient conditioning and stem cell transplants. Lead asset IB-003 targets an inherited anaemia.

**Problem.** Current curative gene therapies for blood disorders are complex, costly and hard on patients. An in-vivo, single-dose approach would reach far more people.

**Team.** Jan Nelis (CEO and co-founder; scientific, entrepreneurial, scale-up and big-pharma experience per the company), Amir Taheri (CSO), and Prof. Jacob Corn (scientific advisor).

**Round.** CHF 2.4M (about $3M) upsized pre-seed on 21 July 2026: CHF 2.25M venture and private capital plus CHF 150K non-dilutive. Led by Backbone Ventures, co-led by OCCIDENT, with Another VC, Kickfund, Venture Kick, Zürcher Kantonalbank, FONGIT, ETH Foundation and private investors.

**Why it made the list.** A real platform-plus-lead-asset story in one of the most fought-over areas of biotech. The proceeds fund preclinical proof-of-concept for IB-003.

**Open questions.** No preclinical data in the announcement. Competition from well-funded in-vivo editors (Beam, Intellia, Verve and others). Delivery is the hard problem, so how "non-viral" delivery performs matters most. CHF 2.4M is thin runway for biotech, so a seed round is likely soon. Coverage disagrees on HQ: the company release says Plan-les-Ouates (Geneva), EU-Startups says Zürich. To confirm.

**Sources.** [Company announcement](https://immitrabio.com/press/2026/20260721_pre-seed_announcement.html) · [EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2026/07/zurich-based-immitra-bio-secures-e2-58-million-to-scale-its-scalable-in-vivo-genetic-medicine-platform/) · [Startupticker](https://www.startupticker.ch/en/news/chf-2-4m-pre-seed-funding-to-advance-immitrabio-scalable-in-vivo-gene-editing-platform-and-lead-asset)

## 3. Allonic (Hungary) · Robotics hardware

**What it does.** A "3D Tissue Braiding" manufacturing platform that builds robot bodies in one automated workflow: a skeletal scaffold, soft load-bearing fibres woven around it, and actuators, tendons, sensors and wiring built in during production. This replaces multi-part assembly.

**Problem.** As one investor put it, AI-driven robot software has outpaced the slow, manual manufacturing of robot hardware, and that gap now limits the industry.

**Team.** Benedek Tasi, Dávid Pelyva and David Holló, founded 2025. Founder backgrounds were not in the coverage I found.

**Round.** €6M ($7.2M) pre-seed on 10 February 2026, reported as Hungary's largest pre-seed ever. Led by Visionaries Club, with Day One Capital, Prototype, SDAC Ventures, TinyVC and angels including people from OpenAI and Hugging Face, plus ETH Zurich and Northwestern University.

**Why it made the list.** A manufacturing-process bet that could benefit any robot maker (humanoid, industrial or medical), with strong signals from the international AI and robotics community.

**Open questions.** No customers, pilots or metrics disclosed. Founder track record unknown. Whether braided bodies match the durability and precision of conventional machined parts. Whether robot makers outsource body manufacturing at all.

**Sources.** [EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2026/02/with-openai-backing-allonic-raises-e6-million-for-robotic-body-manufacturing-platform/) · [Vestbee](https://www.vestbee.com/insights/articles/allonic-secures-7-2-m) · [Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/openai-hugging-face-hungary-biggest-ever-pre-seed)

## 4. Motion (Brussels, Belgium) · Robotics as a service

**What it does.** Europe's first Humanoids-as-a-Service platform, by its own claim. A monthly subscription covers robot selection, task training, IT integration, fleet management, financing, insurance, compliance and maintenance. It is hardware-agnostic, so customers pick the robot best suited to each task.

**Problem.** In the founder's words, adoption fails on financing, risk and compliance before it fails on technology. European factories also face labour shortages.

**Team.** Alexander Stevens (founder and CEO). Other team members not named in the coverage I could access.

**Round.** $2M pre-seed on 27 August 2026, led by Extantia Capital with Norrsken Evolve. Funds move five existing pilots into production and grow the fleet, starting in the Benelux.

**Reported traction.** Five active pilots with industrial, warehouse and logistics companies in Belgium. Tasks include loading crates, packing containers and placing components on conveyors.

**Why it made the list.** A services layer on top of the humanoid boom rather than another hardware bet. Buys robots instead of building them, so the capital need is lower.

**Open questions.** Who owns the fleet and its depreciation risk. Margin after robot cost, insurance and support. Pilot-to-production conversion. Dependence on third-party robot supply and pricing. Whether large integrators or the robot makers themselves take this layer.

**Sources.** [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/08/27/motion-lands-2m-to-expand-humanoid-robot-deployments-across-europe/) · [TFN](https://techfundingnews.com/motion-raises-2m-to-bring-robots-to-european-factories/) · [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/147331-motion-raises-2m-to-deploy-humanoid-robots-across-europe/)

## 5. Tellia (Paris, France and San Francisco, US) · Agriculture

**What it does.** A voice-first assistant for farm teams. Workers call, send voice notes, WhatsApp messages, texts or photos, and the system turns them into structured records tied to the right field, crop and crew: scouting notes, spray records, trial documentation, pest monitoring.

**Problem.** Most growers, including very large operations, still run no farm management software at all, so data capture is the barrier.

**Team.** Coline Labadie de Faÿ (CEO) and Vincent Trastour (CTO). Founded 2024, launched in California in autumn 2025.

**Round.** $5M (€4.3M) pre-seed announced 10 September 2026, led by Revent, with Grey Silo Ventures (venture arm of Cereal Docks Group), Jeriko and Fund F.

**Reported traction.** 80%+ of field teams active daily within two months. Live across about 400,000 hectares (roughly 1M acres) in the US and Europe. Named customers: Campos Brothers Farms, Duckhorn Wineries, IFV, Val de Gascogne and KWS Saat. Revenue model not disclosed.

**Why it made the list.** The best customer evidence in the agtech sample, and already operating in both the US and Europe. Adoption-first design suits a workforce that resists software.

**Open questions.** Revenue and pricing, which are not public. How defensible voice capture is against farm-management platforms adding it. Whether the value is in the data layer or the interface. HQ split: EU-Startups calls it French, other coverage says San Francisco and Paris.

**Sources.** [AgNavigator](https://www.agnavigator.com/Article/2026/09/10/tellia-raises-5m-to-make-farm-data-capture-as-easy-as-a-phone-call/) · [EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2026/09/french-agtech-startup-tellia-raises-e4-3-million-to-scale-voice-enabled-data-capture) · [iGrow News](https://igrownews.com/tellia-latest-news/)

## 6. Backbone (Belgium) · Foodtech

**What it does.** A quality and compliance system for food companies. It pulls supplier documents, lab results and regulatory requirements into one place, with AI agents for certificate tracking, specification checks and incident recording.

**Problem.** Food quality teams run on spreadsheets, documents and email. Data is scattered across departments, so compliance gaps are hard to spot.

**Team.** Louis Opsomer, Siska Lannoo and Julien Steel. Founded 2025. Backgrounds not in the coverage I found.

**Round.** €4M pre-seed on 3 September 2026, led by Pitchdrive with PROfounders Capital, Passion Capital, 100IN and Belgian food-industry investors Agristo and Darta.

**Reported traction.** Used across the food chain (ingredient suppliers, producers, packaging, distributors) in Belgium, the UK and the Netherlands. Customer names and counts not disclosed. Plans partnerships with certification bodies and ERP integrators.

**Why it made the list.** Strategic food-industry investors on the cap table, and compliance software has sticky, recurring revenue. A real foodtech pre-seed in a year when most food money went to later stages.

**Open questions.** Customer numbers and revenue. Competition from incumbents (e.g. FoodDocs, Trace One, SAP-based QMS tools). How much is AI versus workflow software. Note this is the sector's software end, not alternative protein or ingredients.

**Sources.** [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/09/03/backbone-raises-eur4m-for-automated-food-quality-and-compliance/) · [EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2026/09/belgian-food-quality-ai-startup-backbone-raises-e4-million-pre-seed-round/)

## 7. Depotcharge (Munich, Germany) · Logistics and e-mobility services

**What it does.** A manufacturer-neutral network for shared depot charging in electric road freight. Logistics companies open unused charging points at their depots to other fleets. Depotcharge handles marketing, access control, invoicing and payments, and depot owners set price and availability. Operating company is Evolve Energy GmbH.

**Problem.** Reportedly more than 60% of depot charging capacity sits unused, while carriers pay for expensive public charging.

**Team.** Joscha Specks (CEO) and Julius Wilhelm (CPTO), both with 20+ years in e-mobility and earlier work on Digital Charging Solutions (a BMW, Mercedes-Benz and BP joint venture). Marc Clausing joins as CCO.

**Round.** €2.7M pre-seed on 15 September 2026 led by High-Tech Gründerfonds, with xdeck Ventures, Kopa Ventures, Prequel Ventures and angels from logistics, e-mobility and energy.

**Reported traction.** 200+ registered companies and about 600 electric trucks. Active in Germany, Austria, Switzerland and the Benelux. Running ASTAG Charge in Switzerland since May 2026. Distribution partners: BGL (7,000 member companies), E.L.V.I.S. AG (250+ partners at 350+ locations), ASTAG.

**Why it made the list.** An asset-light marketplace with real network traction and industry-association distribution. It tackles the most cited barrier to truck electrification, charging access.

**Open questions.** Take rate and revenue. Whether utilisation is high enough to be worth the depot owners' effort. Competition from public networks and fleet-charging players. Which side of the marketplace is the constraint. Transferability outside DACH.

**Sources.** [HTGF](https://www.htgf.de/en/depotcharge-closes-pre-seed-round/) · [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/09/15/depotcharge-lands-eur27m-and-bgl-partnership-to-scale-electric-truck-charging) · [electrive](https://www.electrive.com/2026/09/15/bgl-and-depotcharge-launch-joint-electric-truck-charging-network/)

## 8. Mycoverse (Denmark) · Foodtech-adjacent: sustainable crop protection

**What it does.** Biological crop protection using fungi in place of chemical pesticides. An AI-driven platform finds high-performing fungal strains, and in-house fungal production technology turns them into products. First target is potato late blight.

**Problem.** Potato late blight is among Europe's most destructive crop diseases. Regulation is pushing down chemical pesticide use and resistance to existing products is growing. The company cites a roughly $1.9B global opportunity.

**Team.** Svend Petersen (CEO) and Niels Bjerg Jensen, a spin-out of the Technical University of Denmark (DTU).

**Round.** €2.4M pre-seed on 3 March 2026, co-led by Future Food Fund and High-Tech Gründerfonds, with PINC (Paulig's venture arm). Total raised about €4.3M including €1.9M earlier from the BioInnovation Institute.

**Reported traction.** Lead candidates performed strongly in greenhouse trials after five months of development. Two years of field trials planned. Partners: DTU and food company Paulig.

**Why it made the list.** Food-supply sustainability with real science behind it, a strategic food-industry investor, and a clear regulatory tailwind. Sits upstream of food rather than in the agtech software category, so it adds a different angle from Tellia.

**Open questions.** Greenhouse results may not carry to the field. Biologicals have a history of inconsistent efficacy and slow registration under EU rules. Single-crop focus at first. Route to market and who distributes. Close to agriculture, so some sector overlap with Tellia.

**Sources.** [HTGF announcement](https://www.htgf.de/en/mycoverse-pre-seed/)

## 9. ORiS (Turin, Italy) · Space and power

**What it does.** Laser-based wireless power transmission, aiming at an in-orbit energy distribution grid for satellites and dual-use applications.

**Problem.** Satellites need more power. Solar panels degrade over time and produce nothing during eclipse, limiting mission life and payload.

**Team.** Four Politecnico di Torino engineers: Andrea Villa (CEO), Anna Mauro (CTO), Domenico Edoardo Sfasciamuro (CCO), Francesco Lopez (COO).

**Round.** €4.5M pre-seed (July 2026) led by Earlybird, co-led by Pitchdrive, with Galaxia, Vento and Piemonte Next Fund, plus a €0.5M regional grant.

**Reported traction.** Powered a hovering drone over 100+ metres on the ground. Completed a lunar wireless-power feasibility study for Thales Alenia Space. Validated a drone-charging platform in NATO's DIANA accelerator. Flight model due for a 2027 in-orbit demo with DCUBED.

**Why it made the list.** Earlybird lead, NATO and Thales validation, and a clear demo milestone in 2027. Dual-use pull from defence.

**Open questions.** Power-transfer efficiency and beam safety. Regulatory path for lasers in orbit. Who buys first: satellite operators, defence, or space agencies. Binary risk on the 2027 demo.

**Sources.** [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/07/21/oris-raises-eur5m-to-build-laser-powered-energy-infrastructure-for-space/) · [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/italian-startup-oris-raises-funding-for-laser-power-beaming-technology/) · [EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2026/07/turin-based-oris-secures-e5-million-to-develop-wireless-power-transmission-systems-for-space-and-dual-use-applications/)

## 10. Telura (Munich, Germany) · Geothermal energy

**What it does.** Electric impulse drilling: high-voltage pulses fracture rock from within rather than grinding it mechanically, targeting deep, hot granite.

**Problem.** Conventional drill bits wear out quickly at 5-20 km depth in hard, hot rock. Drilling can be up to 70% of a geothermal project's cost, which makes many projects uneconomic before they start.

**Team.** Philipp Engelkamp (CEO) and Andrew Welling (CTO). Founded 2025, built on about two decades of German university research.

**Round.** €4M pre-seed raised autumn 2025 from Nucleus Capital, Possible Ventures and First Momentum. Announced March 2026.

**Reported traction.** Demonstrated granite-breaking efficiency in research settings. Validation agreement with SPRIND, Germany's breakthrough-innovation agency. Market entry planned for 2026.

**Why it made the list.** If drilling cost falls, superhot-rock geothermal becomes a baseload option, and that is a large prize. US interest in next-generation geothermal (Fervo, Quaise) is strong.

**Open questions.** Distance from lab to field-scale drilling. Rate of penetration versus alternatives such as Quaise's millimetre-wave approach. Capital needs before revenue. The round is about a year old, so a seed raise may be near.

**Sources.** [EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2026/03/germanys-telura-exits-stealth-with-e4-million-pre-seed-to-unlock-geothermal-energy-with-electric-impulse-drilling/)

## 11. finperks (Berlin, Germany) · Software and fintech

**What it does.** A single API into 1,000+ European brands for the prepaid market: brand-funded cashback, digital employee benefits (Germany's €50/month tax-free benefit), instant digital gift cards.

**Problem.** Prepaid infrastructure is fragmented. Partners have to integrate several vendors across issuing, brand relationships, settlement and compliance.

**Team.** Achim Bönsch, Sebastian Seifert, Andreas Veller. All three built Barzahlen/viafintech, sold to NYSE-listed Paysafe in a reported nine-figure deal in 2021.

**Round.** €3.4M ($4M) pre-seed (March 2026) led by Motive Partners and seed+speed Ventures.

**Reported traction (about six months after launch).** Live with the payment app Flizpay (average 5% brand-funded cashback) and HR platforms Recardy and Paylo. Company cites a €1.2T market.

**Why it made the list.** Proven founders with a prior exit in the same payments neighbourhood. Motive Partners is a fintech specialist with US and UK reach.

**Open questions.** Thin margin structure in prepaid and gift cards. Regulatory dependence on the German employee-benefit rule. Whether "Stripe for prepaid" scales beyond Germany. The €1.2T figure needs a source.

**Sources.** [EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2026/03/ex-viafintech-founders-new-venture-finperks-raises-e3-4-million-pre-seed-to-build-the-stripe-for-prepaid/)
